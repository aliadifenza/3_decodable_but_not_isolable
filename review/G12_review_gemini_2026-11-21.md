## Fidelity Check (§1): G5 Narrowing vs. V1.1

* **Verdict: Narrowing is a design choice, not a hypothesis change—but it creates a scope mismatch that weakens claims about V1.1.**


* Protocol V1.1 explicitly allows a Form to be a *"multidimensional, distributed structure"* across manifestations and roles. Freezing a greedy set of **≤ 8 SAE latents** at a **single token** is a severe operational narrowing. Calling it a "design narrowing" rather than a change of hypothesis is fair *if* the experiment is framed as testing a specific sparse-feature mechanism. It becomes problematic if a G5 null is treated as refuting V1.1's broader philosophical picture rather than refuting *sparse, single-token SAE latent necessity under G5 rules*.

## Claim-by-Claim Audit (§6)

### Claim 1 `[HIGH]` — *"Gemma-2-2B represents entity identity (kind vs property-matched look-alikes, across descriptions and roles) strongly and linearly at the reference position, layers 2–14."*

* **Rating:** **Supported (with caveats on data provenance and generalization).**


* **Why:** Same-site probe AUCs of ~0.93–1.00 across layers 2–14 and downstream AUCs of 0.70–0.94 support strong linear decodability of entity kind. However, validation/eval splits were generated by Claude from templates (potential style/template cues), and "across descriptions and roles" needs careful wording: role-probe *drop* was ~0 under interventions, which shows interventions didn't disrupt role separability, not necessarily that a single linear identity subspace is invariant across every grammatical/role framing without artifact.



### Claim 2 `[HIGH]` — *"Under the frozen rules, no layer qualified; P2 (Substance → Causality log-odds dependency) and P3 (mandatory pathway) are inconclusive — not supported, not refuted. The evaluation set remains sealed."*

* **Rating:** **Supported.**


* **Why:** R1 failed across all 156 layer×kind pairs (`r ≈ 0.00`), R3 mostly failed due to low baseline item eligibility (e.g., 0/8 for can, 2/8 for egg), and R2 only passed sporadically. Under the pre-registered G5 gate, keeping P2/P3 inconclusive and the evaluation set sealed is the only disciplined call.



### Claim 3 `[HIGH, exploratory]` — *"The G5r null is not a dose or placement problem: ablating up to 128 identity-selected SAE latents, at one or all reference tokens, removes ≈ 0 downstream identity; random active latents with ~5× larger edits also remove ≈ 0."*

* **Rating:** **Overstated.**


* **Why:** "Not a dose or placement problem" overreaches what E1 and E2 tested. E1 still used **SAE latents** (and greedy selection from a top-512 pool on a worst-stratum AUC criterion). Even at `k = 128` across all tokens, median `‖Δ‖/‖h‖` is only **0.064** (`r = 0.005`). You haven't tested a large dose in residual space (e.g., ablating/perturbing top-k PCA/SVD components or larger dense subspaces), nor have you tested post-reference / downstream positions. Saying "≈ 0 downstream identity" is roughly true at the median (`r = 0.005`), though the random active latent control already shows wider upper spread (`IQR` up to `0.069`, `‖Δ‖/‖h‖ = 0.302`).



### Claim 4 `[MED, exploratory]` — *"Erasing a single linear identity direction from the full residual (including SAE error) makes identity linearly unrecoverable at the site but removes only ~15–35% (median) of identity read downstream; a matched random direction removes ≈ 0. ⇒ identity is carried by the representation in a way that sparse SAE latents miss and that the model largely reconstitutes downstream."*

* **Rating:** **Overstated (especially the `⇒` inference and "unrecoverable at the site").**


* **Why:**
1. **"Unrecoverable at the site"** is too strong based on `amnesic AUC = 0.500` on the fitting/intervention distribution (see §7a).


2. **The `⇒` claim ("carried ... in a way that sparse SAE latents miss and that the model largely reconstitutes downstream")** packs two unverified causal stories into one arrow: (i) SAE miss (expected given Gemma Scope 16k has `L0 ≈ 70` and SAE reconstruction error isn't zero), and (ii) downstream "reconstitution" (which could just be information already copied earlier or present elsewhere; see §7b).


3. Also, the median `r` across E3 conditions ranges from **0.180 to 0.261** (with IQRs spanning ~0.12 to 0.35, and L6 hitting 0.55). Writing "~15–35% (median)" is a bit loose when two main conditions sit at 0.180 and 0.261.





### Claim 5 `[LOW, exploratory]` — *"This pattern is consistent with the PI's description of identity as an organized, distributed whole ('organized togetherness; no single part essential') and with V1.1's 'multidimensional, distributed' Form — but it is not evidence for it (see §7)."*

* **Rating:** **Overstated / Risky framing.**


* **Why:** Even with the explicit disclaimer ("not evidence for it") and `[LOW]` tag, calling a weak, noisy partial linear erasure (`r ≈ 0.18–0.26`, mostly missing the R1 bar, with unruled-out attention/leakage confounds) "consistent with" a rich philosophical construct like *organized togetherness* invites readers to treat poetry/philosophy as corroborated by noisy linear algebra. Better to state strictly what it is consistent with mathematically/mechanistically (distributed/redundant linear encoding across residual dimensions rather than a single sparse SAE feature) and leave philosophical alignment to a clearly separated discussion note.



### Claim 6 `[HIGH]` — *"No conclusion about Substance → Causality is possible from the exploratory D comparison (≤ 5 items, effects ≤ 0.1 log-odds)."*

* **Rating:** **Supported.**


* **Why:** Sample sizes (3–5 items vs. the 30-item / 12-event minimum) and effect sizes (`≤ 0.1` vs. the `0.5` log-odds minimum effect of interest, with CIs largely straddling zero and no magnitude matching) are far below any credible evidentiary threshold.

## Answers to Specific Questions & Added Weaknesses (§7)

### 7a. Amnesic AUC = 0.50 & "unrecoverable at the site"

* **Is "unrecoverable at the site" overstated?** **Yes.**


* An amnesic AUC of `0.500` under a freshly fit linear probe after mean-difference/LEACE projection is largely an algebraic/optimization consequence on the erased training distribution—not proof that identity information is information-theoretically or non-linearly gone at that site, nor that it wouldn't be linearly separable under a different sampling/evaluation split or non-linear readout. Phrase it as *"drops linear probe accuracy at the site to chance on the tested split"* rather than *"unrecoverable."*



### 7b. Early/parallel information movement & impact on Claims 4–5

* **How much does this undermine Claims 4–5?** **Substantially for the "reconstitution" story; moderately for the "distributed representation" observation.**


* If earlier layers (`< L`) have already routed entity/context cues to other token positions or downstream residual streams—or if the downstream probe reads tokens/features that did not pass through the exact erased bottleneck at layer `L`—then surviving downstream decodability does **not** prove active "reconstitution" by a distributed Form. Without ablating/erasing at *all* relevant positions or at the downstream read site/pathway, "reconstitution" is an interpretation, not a demonstration.



### 7c. Ratio `r` with small denominator

* When clean downstream AUC is already modest (`0.70–0.76`), `AUC_clean - 0.5` is small (`0.20–0.26`). Small noise or sampling variance in `AUC_ablated` gets divided by a small number, inflating `r` (like the `0.55` spike at L6) and blowing up bootstrap CIs (upper bounds `> 1` mean the point estimate of `AUC_ablated` dipped below `0.5`, which is noisy estimation rather than "more than 100% removal"). Always report raw clean vs. ablated AUCs alongside `r` and cap/flag noisy ratio intervals.



### 7d. Why mean-difference (`r = 0.261`, 7/156 meeting bar) beats LEACE (`r = 0.180`, 0/156) despite cosine = 1.00

* Even when the **direction** is collinear (`cosine = 1.00`), **step size / projection geometry** differs:
1. **10% covariance shrinkage in LEACE** alters the whitening/orthogonalization metric relative to raw mean-difference projection, changing how much variance along (and slightly off-axis from) the mean-difference direction is removed—and changing `‖Δ‖/‖h‖` (`0.144` for LEACE vs. `0.200` for mean-difference).


2. **Mean-difference projection** removes the full mean shift along that vector directly, whereas regularized/shrunk LEACE leaves residual variance along the concept direction when covariance is ill-conditioned or shrunk. Check `‖Δ‖/‖h‖` per layer and run LEACE with `0%` shrinkage vs. unshrunk mean-difference projection to separate shrinkage from projection mechanics.



### 7e. Is E1 a fair dose test, or did selection pick rare latents?

* **It is a fair test of *sparse SAE feature ablation under the G5 selection rule*, but a weak test of *high-dose representational perturbation*.**


* Selecting via worst-stratum AUC/ablation on a top-512 pool can favor features that are diagnostic on a hard subset rather than high-norm or high-frequency drivers, and SAE latents have low `‖Δ‖/‖h‖` (`0.053` at `k=8`, `0.064` at `k=128` vs. `0.302` for random active latents). Notice the contradiction in the random active control: `‖Δ‖/‖h‖ = 0.302` is large, yet `r = 0.015`. That suggests even big edits *within the SAE active subspace* (or off the causal path) don't move downstream identity much—which is useful, as long as you don't conflate SAE latent count `k` with total representational dose.



### 7f. Data threats to Claims 1–4

* **Claude-generated validation/eval templates:** High risk of **template/lexical shortcuts** inflating probe separability (Claim 1) and potentially shifting distribution relative to PI-curated discovery v1.2 (affecting transfer of probes/thresholds in Claims 3–4). Audit whether probes are decoding entity semantics vs. surface phrasing/templates.
* **Low causal item eligibility (0/8 to 5/8):** Makes causal claims (P2/P3 and Claim 6) heavily underpowered and potentially biased toward whichever few items happened to pass baseline eligibility.

### 7g. Selection effects & the "exploratory" label

* Reporting all layers 2–14 avoids cherry-picking layers, and keeping E1–E3 separate from P2/P3/evaluation is good hygiene. However, **running post-hoc follow-ups after seeing G5r and highlighting cherry-picked wins** (like L6 `r = 0.55` or the 9/156 cases meeting the R1 bar, where 7/9 didn't even pass in the main table row or had huge CIs) still carries post-hoc selection bias. Keep the `[exploratory]` label everywhere, avoid highlighting single-cell peaks as headline effects, and treat any E1–E3 finding as a hypothesis for a future preregistered run—not a settled result.



### 7h. Overall headline

* Your proposed headline (*"identity is present but not removable by sparse SAE ablation; linear erasure removes it locally but it largely returns downstream; the Kantian dependency remains untested"*) is close, but **"largely returns downstream"** overstates a median `r` of `0.18–0.26`.


* **Suggested alternative:**
> *"Entity identity is linearly decodable in Gemma-2-2B (L2–L14), but sparse SAE ablation fails to remove it downstream; linear mean-difference erasure only partially reduces downstream identity (median r ≈ 0.18–0.26), and Kantian Substance → Causality remains untested."*

## Additional Weaknesses & Mismatches to Flag Before 13:00 CET

1. **P2/P3 vs. R1 metric conflation in E1–E3:** The original plan defines P2 via **target log-odds** (`D = L_intact - L_intervened`, Δ1, Δ2) and P3 via **pathway-specific disruption + restoration**, whereas E1–E3 primarily evaluate **probe AUC / removal ratio `r**` (with an underpowered, magnitude-unmatched D check). Make sure readers don't mistake downstream probe drop `r` for causal task/log-odds impact `D`.


2. **Missing magnitude matching in the optional D check (§4):** Skipping magnitude matching (deviation from Decision 2) on a tiny, noisy set (`n ≤ 5`, effects `≤ 0.1` vs. `0.5` threshold) means even the sign of Δ1/Δ2 is unreliable. Cut or heavily caveate those numbers in the submission so they don't clutter a clean null/inconclusive story.


3. **Dataset asymmetry (PI discovery v1.2 vs. Claude-templated val/eval):** Whenever a probe is trained on discovery and evaluated on Claude-generated validation templates (or vice versa), template artifacts can masquerade as cross-split generalization. Run a quick sanity check on whether accuracy differs between descriptions and derived noun sentences, or across templates, before submitting.
